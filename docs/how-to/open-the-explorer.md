# Open the explorer

Read the dossiers in your browser, from the [map](arrange-the-map.md)'s groups and components down to each claim and the evidence behind it. The explorer reads the dossiers root and never changes it. It shows what `dossier verify` concludes and computes no standing of its own.

## Steps

1. **Build the page once.** Ask the agent to open the explorer, which runs the explore skill, or run `dossier explore --install` yourself. The agent asks before it downloads anything. The command downloads the page's packages from the npm registry, at the versions the plugin pins, builds the page into `.explorer/` under the dossiers root and removes the packages. The folder ignores itself, so git does not see it. After the plugin is updated, `dossier explore` says the page was built from other sources, and `--install` builds it again.

2. **Start it.** Run `dossier explore`, or let the agent start it. It serves the page at `http://127.0.0.1:3677/` until you stop it with Ctrl-C. When that port is taken it takes the next free one, and `--port` picks another. It answers only on this machine, and only to a page opened at that address.

3. **Find your way.** The spine on the left is the [map](arrange-the-map.md) at the top of the root's `README.md`: its groups, nested to any depth, and the components at their leaves, each holding the dossiers read there. A dot beside each node stands for the claim most at stake beneath it, and pointing at it gives the counts in words. The dot shows standing only, so a node with every claim supported can still have a review owed beneath it. The page opens on the root, which the title in the top bar opens again from anywhere; the spine begins with the root's groups. The root and each group list what they hold, each with its lead and its claims counted by standing, beside their featured readings and the compositions that sit there. An Attention line counts what an owner, a reviewer or the agent must act on beneath the page. A dossier no component holds is listed under Not placed, and on a root without a map every dossier is.

4. **Read a component.** A component's page merges the dossiers it holds, taking from a dossier held in part only the behaviors and claims its selection names. Behaviors lists the claims that describe what happens, headed by dossier when the component holds more than one. Guarantees lists its requirements, the claims of kind `requirement`, each with its assumptions. Relies on lists what the parts it holds whole rely on: each [Guarantee passage](back-a-guarantee.md), with its backing `backed`, `assumed` or `unresolved`, and each Given, a written assumption about an actor. Flows lists the compositions its whole dossiers take part in, and those that sit there. A section with nothing in it is left out, so a component with no requirement shows no Guarantees. A dossier's own page reads the same way, with the components that hold it named at the top.

5. **See why a claim holds.** Select a claim to open Why this holds beside the page, or beneath it in a narrow window. It reads in four steps. Source gives what the claim is about, the records it pins and its assumptions. Run gives each mapping's target and what each run showed. Independent review gives each reviewer's assessment. Owner gives the owner's decision. Each step opens the record or the Workbench view it comes from, and Escape closes the panel.

6. **See what needs you.** The Attention lens lists only what an owner, a reviewer or the agent must act on beneath the page you are on: tripwires, decisions owed, contradicted and contested claims, open doubts, evidence awaiting review, stale and owed reviews, and map notes. A violation the owner accepted is listed apart, last. Where a decision settles an item, such as answering a [doubt](../reference/glossary.md) or accepting a risk, the explorer prepares the `dossier decide` line with your words left blank. Fill them in and run it, or ask the agent to record it, as [Record a decision](record-a-decision.md) describes. A stale or owed review needs a [second read](get-a-second-read.md), not a decision. On a root without a map, Attention says so once, with the `dossier place` line that starts one.

7. **Open the tools.** The Workbench lens keeps the deep views. At a node, it lists every dossier beneath it under its [next step](../reference/marks.md#the-next-step), from `Fix` to `Report`, with its [flags](../reference/marks.md#flags) and [tripwires](../reference/marks.md#tripwires). For a dossier, Claims gives each claim's scope, mappings, [marks](../reference/marks.md), runs, reviews, decisions, limits and raw record, then the dossier's reviews and, under No behavior, what no behavior maps to. Chart gives a part's prose beside its statechart, synced both ways, the grid of states by events and the walks as lanes. System gives a composition's members, wiring and actors, and each property under each [scenario](../explanation/parts-compositions-scenarios.md#actors-modes-and-scenarios).

8. **Play what happened.** On a claim's walk, Play steps through the walk's recorded witness. Where none is recorded, it replays the walk on the chart, which adds no evidence. In Chart, the Play tab replays a walk, either reading of a doubt, or the trace an answer holds, step by step on the chart, with the passage that decides each step. Simulate sends any event from any explored state and shows each condition evaluated. Find a path searches the explored states for one you describe.

⌘K or Ctrl K goes to a group, component, dossier, behavior, claim, passage, doubt, walk, cell or answer. `r`, `a` and `w` switch between Read, Attention and Workbench, and `?` lists the keys and the words each lens uses beside the terms the tools print. Every view and selection has its own address.

The page follows the files. A change under the root, such as a new answer, an edited passage or a new placement, or in the knowledge base, refreshes it. The ribbon at the top lists the marks that moved, and when you open the page, what moved since your last visit.

The views are bounded. The spine and every page are lists, the largest graph is one composition, and a graph with more than 25 boxes is refused rather than drawn.
