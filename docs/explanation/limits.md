# The limits of a mark

A mark is a statement about the model. It holds as far as the trial behind it looked, and no further. The method makes carelessness visible and the guards refuse the obvious shortcuts. Neither makes a mark say more than these limits allow.

## Behavior collections

`dossier verify` resolves [behavior claims](../../spec/behaviors.md) against the current evidence and [scoped reviews](../../spec/review.md). A run that passes means verify found no tripwire. It does not mean that every claim is supported, that every review is complete or that every behavior of the source is covered. Read a claim's scope, freshness, assessment and limitations together.

A behavior's explanation and its mappings to evidence require judgment. A scoped review records that judgment, and it may be incomplete or wrong. A list of every modeled event does not prove that the code or a deployment is covered in full. An untested hypothesis stays an open question. [Behaviors and claims](behaviors.md#how-a-claim-earns-its-standing) says what each standing needs.

## Evidence limits

- **A proof is about the abstraction.** `proved` is a theorem about the model the prose compiles to, under one scenario. It is only as sound as the guarantees it rests on, which each part ledger lists with their backing. The assumed ones are where the risk is. Whether the abstraction is faithful to the system is the owner's judgment, made with backings and replays as evidence. A model can pass every check and still state the wrong requirement or leave out behavior that matters.
- **A backing is a name, not a proof.** `backed` says a rule with that name exists at that commit. Whether the rule proves the guarantee as the prose words it is a judgment the agent writes down and a reviewer checks.
- **Scenarios are about the model's actors.** A checked cell under a malicious governance key says the procedure as written cannot reach a bad state within the reach, when the key does whatever the contracts allow. It says nothing about whether the real signers behave that way, or how likely the key is to fall into malicious hands.
- **A malicious actor has only the powers the model gives it.** Malicious drops an actor's Givens, so it can do anything the modeled contracts allow, at any time, within the ranges the boundary declares. It can do nothing the model leaves out. Capital that exists only within one transaction, such as a flash loan, or a price pushed in a market the model does not include, is checked only once someone models it.
- **Givens are assumptions, never checked.** A property that cites a Given rests on it, such as a Within that needs a keeper to act before the next block. The record shows which result rests on which Given, and a scenario shows what happens without it. Whether the keeper goes on acting depends on incentives and infrastructure the model does not hold.
- **Bounded checks are bounded.** `checked` means no violation within the reach, or no counterexample within the scope. A reach is a choice, and every mark shows it. `proved` lifts the bound on steps and keeps the model's ranges and constants. A system with several actors and realistic ranges is too large to explore, so models start from small ranges and abstractions, and a check is widened once it passes.
- **The chart has no Givens.** It checks the prose against itself over small ranges. An Always with no Given that the chart rejects is not an Always, whatever a model says under fairness. An Always that cites a Given is skipped by the chart, and only the solvers check it, so in a charted part it stays `untried`.
- **Nothing refuses an edit.** The record is protected by showing changes, not by preventing them. Integrity checks detect changed or missing records against retained bindings. They cannot detect a consistent rewrite of every record and anchor.
- **Local records do not authenticate their authors.** Hashes neither prove owner identity nor independently prove honest execution. An entire deleted dossier has no remaining per-dossier manifest to report its absence. Keep version history or an external inventory. [Storage](../../spec/storage.md#read-only-verification-and-trust-limits) states this boundary.
- **The record holds answers, not the checker's output.** Each answer is the runner's account of what the checker returned. The checker's own output stays in the trial's [bundle](../reference/files.md#trial-bundles), on the machine that ran the trial. Verify never reads a bundle. A clone of the repository holds only the answers, which [`dossier recheck`](evidence.md#one-answer-per-question) confirms where the toolchain they came from is installed.
- **Older answers live in version control.** A dossier keeps the current answer to each question and the last one that differed. A trial that gives the same answer again leaves the entry unchanged, so the record does not show how often a question was asked. Earlier answers are only in version control, and rewriting its history, such as squashing commits, removes them.
- **Private inputs do not freeze the whole environment.** Declared files and supported local imports are captured. Toolchains, shared libraries and an external runner's surroundings are not. A replay supports its recorded scope, not every possible state of a live deployment.
- **Coverage is mechanical.** An answer covers a passage when it would have come back otherwise had the passage been false, and rules over the answer decide which passages that is. The rules catch a property that was never enabled and an action that never fired. They cannot catch a property that is faithful in form and empty in substance. Only a blunder does, and the agent chooses the blunders. A second read with the review skill is the check on exactly that.
- **Verify reads only part of a model.** It checks that actions, fairness clauses and Alloy facts cite passages. It does not read a model's `Init` or a state constraint in a TLC configuration, and `narrowed` compares only the numbers an answer records as its reach. A restriction added there shrinks what a trial explores without a flag, and only a second read catches it.
- **Only the second read checks substance.** Verify checks the form of what the agent writes, such as a passage cited by every model element and a classification on every [miscall](evidence.md#a-call-before-every-trial). Whether a property says what its passage says, a scenario means what its column says, or a `call was wrong` was really that, is a judgment. [The review contract](../../spec/review.md) lists the judgments [a second read](../how-to/get-a-second-read.md) makes. A review binds the behaviors in its scope, with their formal targets and evidence. That scope is one behavior, for a focused review, or the whole dossier. Verify checks those bindings and reports any input that changed. It cannot establish that the reviewer's judgment is correct.
- **Review has a declared scope.** A reviewer must inspect the source or deployment evidence a claim depends on, and must name what it did not examine. A behavior outside that scope stays unchecked. No tool checks the owners' words in a decision or an accepted risk. The runner checks a call's prediction, not its reason on the `because:` line.
- **A declared `stronger_than` is checked by its form.** Every actor's mode in the stronger scenario must be the same or malicious, its windows must agree, and both must start from the same values. That is a conservative rule for a semantic question: it refuses what it cannot show, and a true strengthening it cannot see trips `stronger_than unsound` with no override. Reword the scenarios so the rule can see it.
- **Nothing runs in the background.** A lapse shows the next time `dossier verify` runs, not when the change happens. The agent runs verify first whenever it works on the dossiers.
- **One knowledge base, one dossiers root.** A seam to a part in another repository stays owed.
- **Parallel regions draw but do not scaffold.** A part with parallel regions is modeled by hand.
