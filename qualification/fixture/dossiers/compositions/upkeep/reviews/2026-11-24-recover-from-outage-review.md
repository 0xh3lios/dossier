**F1. The two outage windows differ in whether they reach a stale price, not in their length. The account built on them reads the one-block cell as tolerance of a one-block outage, which it does not show.** Verified. The finding covers the model and these two cells.

- lanes-down-one takes both lanes offline only while the clock is 0 (scenarios.md lines 24-28; Upkeep.tla lines 31, 51-52). In that block the Oracle starts Fresh (lines 54-56) and every post is refused (lines 62-66; parts/oracle/prose.md line 23). The tick that makes the price stale also ends the window, as the answer's own call says. Its state and action counts equal those of `tlc-N1-all-honest-fdfe6f`: 94 generated, 31 distinct, depth 8, and per action Lane_a 16 and 38, Lane_b 0 and 38, TickStep 14 and 17.
- lanes-down-two misses the deadline at the tick taken at clock 1 (trace states 2 to 3). That is its second block, and the first in which the price is stale.

The following read the cells as tolerance of a one-block outage, which the cells do not show:

- the explanation's "Outage duration changes whether the same deadline can be met" (behavior line 125);
- T1's "Down for one block they recover in time, down for two they do not" (prose.md line 20);
- the reading's "one block of outage is survivable and two are not" (`tlc-N1-lanes-down-two-2770b9.md` line 1);
- the second clause of the owner's risk words, "one block is what the lanes are sized for" (decisions.log line 3).

The owner's decision is the owner's, and this finding changes no decision.

**F2. A one-block outage that begins once the price is stale would miss the deadline.** Not verified. By reading the module: if both lanes are offline at any clock of 1 or more while the Oracle is Stale, no post is due, so the chain may tick while Stale (lines 102-107). That is what the lanes-down-two trace does at clock 1. But no scenario declares a one-block window that starts at tick 1, so no answer records it. The missing evidence is a cell under such a scenario. I ran no trial.

**F3. No answer and no walk shows a recovery after an outage during which the price was stale (formal:19).** Verified.

The explanation says "A prompt lane can refresh after availability returns" (behavior line 127), but:

- in lanes-down-two, TLC stopped at the violation inside the window (TypeOK is partial, depth 3);

- the composition has no walk, and verify --json reports `walks: []`.

**F4. stale-start's age value has no effect, and the deadline is measured from the start (formal:12).** Verified.

- The start sets stale to 3, but nothing on the honest path reads stale (Upkeep.tla lines 62-90, 97, 124). M2 and M3 fire only on a tick, and a tick is disabled in Stale while a lane is honest (lines 102-107).
- Init sets `n1_ticks` to 0 in a state that is already Stale (line 59), so the three earlier stale blocks do not count against N1. N1 would even allow one tick before the refresh.

The cell supports C4 as worded. It does not exercise the saturation that the member's M3 and D1 concern. T1's "a price already three blocks stale is refreshed before the next block" holds in the model only because the prompt clause blocks the tick, not because N1 requires it.

**F5. The honest-lane N1 cells (C2 after its window, C4) check that the chain is held back, not that a post refreshes the Oracle.** Verified, by reading the module.

- Under these scenarios, then, N1 cannot fail, whatever state a post leads to.
